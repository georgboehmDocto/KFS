KFS

Korean Firing Sport

Rules of play

Two teams of two. Two tables with 30–40 centimetres between them. Six cups a side, stacked at the edge each team sits behind. Bounce a ping-pong ball off your own table, then off theirs, and take their pyramid down.

Article 1Table, cups, kit

Players
Two teams of two
Cups
Six per team, plus one water cup
Balls
Two ping-pong balls per turn; one tennis ball, thrown as a reward
Gear
One badminton racket per team; one toy gun
Officials
One ref, who is not playing
1.1

Two long tables of the same height stand with their long sides parallel, separated by a gap of 30–40 cm.

1.2

Each team owns one table and sits at its outer long edge, facing the gap and the other team.

1.3

Each team stacks exactly six cups upside down at the edge of its own table, directly in front of where it sits: three cups side by side as level 1, two resting in the grooves as level 2, one on top as level 3.

1.4

Each team fills one further cup with water, almost to the brim, and stands it upright on the gap side of the pyramid, touching the middle cup of level 1. This is the water cup (Article 6).

1.5

Both players sit immediately behind their own pyramid, on chairs or a bench.

1.6

Each team receives exactly one badminton racket at the start of the game.

1.7

One person who is not playing acts as ref (Article 9) and holds the toy gun.

Article 2Starting a game

2.1

Before anything else, the two teams meet, shake hands, bow, and say 안녕하세요 — annyeonghaseyo, hello.

2.2

Each team then offers the first turn to the other. Handing over the opening throws is the first show of respect in a game built on it, and refusing to back down from that offer is the stronger move. Whichever team accepts throws first.

2.3

Both teams return behind their pyramids. When both are ready, the ref counts down in Korean — 셋, 둘, 하나 (set, dul, hana) — and fires the toy gun. The game is live on the shot.

2.4

The team throwing first may release on the gunshot itself. That is not disrespectful and may not be treated as such.

2.5

Opening the game with a direct throw (3.5), after being handed the first turn, is bad manners. It is not disrespect under Article 10 and no foul may be called for it. It is simply remembered.

Article 3Throwing

3.1

Teams throw in alternating turns. A turn is two ping-pong balls.

3.2

The balls may be thrown at the same time or one after the other, and both may be thrown by the same player. Over a game, the throws should even out between the two players of a team.

Clean throws and blockable throws

No throw in KFS is forbidden. The only question a throw answers is whether the other team is allowed to touch it.

3.3

A throw is clean if it bounces exactly once on the thrower's own table, then exactly once on the opponent's table, and then either reaches the cups or leaves the far table over a side or the back edge. Nothing that happens after the second bounce makes a throw unclean. A ball in the gap is never clean (5.2).

3.4

Every other throw is blockable: a missing bounce, an extra bounce, a ball thrown straight at the pyramid. Throwing one is not an offence and carries no penalty. It can simply be slapped out of the air (Article 4).

3.5

The direct throw — straight at the pyramid, no bounce at all — is a standard tactic among experienced teams, and blockable like any other.

3.6

Two direct throws may never be released at the same time, whether one from each player or both from the same player. That is foul play (5.8). Direct throws arrive one at a time so that they can always be answered — the game rewards a defender's reaction, not two balls at once and an argument with the ref. A direct throw released at the same moment as a clean throw is fine, and sometimes the right move — though doing it turn after turn is disrespect, and the ref may treat it as such.

Cups that go over

3.7

Cups count however they were knocked over, by a clean throw or a blockable one.

3.8

Every cup knocked over is removed from the table immediately, and it makes no difference who knocked it over. A team's own cups count against it however they fall — a throw, a block, an elbow, a shoved table. Cups are never put back except under 5.8 and 8.4.

3.9

If a knocked cup comes to rest on top of another cup, both cups count as knocked over and both are removed.

3.10

A ball that arrives and knocks nothing over has no effect. The turn ends once the team's second ball has been thrown and come to rest.

Article 4Blocking

4.1

The team being thrown at may slap away any blockable throw (3.4), with a bare hand or with its racket.

4.2

The block has to be immediate, while the ball is in flight. Once a ball has connected with a cup it cannot be blocked.

4.3

Slapping away a clean throw is a foul (Article 5). Reading the bounces correctly, in the moment, is the defender's whole job.

4.4

Any of its own cups the defending team knocks over while blocking — with hand, racket, arm or body — counts as knocked over and is removed at once, under the general rule of 3.8. The stacking rule of 3.9 applies to those cups too.

4.5

Slapping a ball into the gap is a foul, whatever kind of throw it was (5.3).

Article 5Fouls

5.1

Every foul has the same consequence: the other team gets one throw with the tennis ball (Article 6). There is no other penalty in the game.

A foul is always charged to one team and awarded to the other. The general rule for the gap: whoever put the ball in it is at fault.

5.2

The throwing team puts a ball in the gap between the tables: foul, awarded to the defending team.

5.3

The defending team slaps a ball into the gap: foul, awarded to the throwing team.

5.4

The defending team blocks a clean throw (4.3): foul, awarded to the throwing team.

5.5

A tennis ball put into the gap: foul, and the other team gets the tennis throw instead (6.3).

5.6

The ref may call a foul on any illegal play. This is meant to be the exception, not the norm.

5.7

Repeated disrespect toward the other team is a foul like any other (Article 10).

5.8

Two direct throws released at the same time, from either or both players (3.6): foul, awarded to the defending team, and any cups those two balls knocked over are restored.

Article 6Tennis ball and water cup

There is one reward in KFS and it is the tennis ball. A team earns a throw with it two ways: by being fouled (5.1), or by putting a ping-pong ball in the other team's water cup.

The tennis throw

6.1

The tennis throw is taken immediately, the moment it is earned — in the middle of a turn, in the middle of a last stand, whenever. The ball may then be thrown however the team likes: bounces, direct, overarm, along the table. 3.3 does not apply to it.

6.2

The tennis ball cannot be blocked by anyone, by hand or by racket.

6.3

If the tennis ball goes into the gap, that is a foul, and the other team gets the tennis throw instead.

6.4

Cups the tennis ball knocks over count as usual, including the stacking rule of 3.9. It is a tennis ball hitting a wall of plastic cups, so expect it to be decisive.

The water cup

Some teams play as if the water cup were decoration. It is the fastest way to lose.

6.5

A ping-pong ball that lands in the opponent's water cup earns the throwing team a tennis throw.

6.6

If a team lands a ball in its own water cup, or slaps a blockable throw into its own water cup while defending, it counts as though the opponent had made the cup: the opponent gets the tennis throw.

6.7

However a water cup is made, it goes straight back to the same spot, refilled. The table is wiped so that balls still bounce true.

6.8

There is no limit to how often a water cup can be made in one game.

Article 7Three cups or fewer

When a team is knocked down to three cups or fewer, the game changes shape. This happens once per team per game, at the moment the threshold is crossed, and it is the attacking team that decides what happens.

7.1

The attacking team is asked what it wants: the remaining cups rebuilt into a 2-1 pyramid — two side by side, one on top — or left standing exactly where they are. If it does not answer, the cups are rebuilt.

7.2

If a 2-1 pyramid cannot be built from what is left, the cups keep their shape, but they are still pushed together until they touch and centred on the edge.

7.3

The water cup moves, whether the cups were rebuilt or not. The attacking team places it anywhere it likes on that table and must move it at least a little — a centimetre is enough, but standing pat is not an option. A small pyramid shielded by a water cup is close to unhittable, which is the whole reason this rule exists.

7.4

The threshold does not fire again. A team that drops from three cups to two, or that climbs back above three after a last stand (8.4), goes through none of this a second time.

Article 8Winning: the last stand

Losing your last cup does not lose you the game. It buys you exactly one turn.

8.1

When a team loses its last cup, count the cups it had standing before that final turn — the same as the number that just fell. Call it n, usually one or two.

8.2

The team with no cups left takes one turn: the last stand. It must knock over at least n cups from the team that just finished it off.

8.3

If it knocks over fewer than n, the game is over. The team with cups left on the table wins.

8.4

If it knocks over n or more, every cup it knocked over is removed from the opponent. The team coming back then puts up the smaller of two numbers: the cups it just knocked over, or n. Answering one cup with four restores one cup, not four — though all four still leave the opponent's table. Answering three with three restores three.

8.5

A last stand is an ordinary turn in every other respect. Blockable throws can be slapped away, clean throws may not be touched, water cups can be made, fouls can be committed, tennis throws can be earned and used.

8.6

A team that survives a last stand and is later emptied again gets another one, on the same terms. There is no limit.

8.7

When the game ends, both teams shake hands, bow, and say 감사합니다 — gamsahamnida, thank you.

Article 9The ref

9.1

KFS is fast and often chaotic. When it is unclear which rules apply after an exchange, the ref decides. The decision is made without debate and is final.

9.2

A team that refuses to follow the ref's decision can expect one foul, and another, until it does.

9.3

The ref may call fouls on illegal play, but should use this sparingly (5.4).

9.4

The ref runs the opening countdown and holds the toy gun (2.2).

Article 10Respect

10.1

KFS is a game of respect before it is a game of cups. The bow and the handshake at both ends of the game are part of the rules, not a garnish.

10.2

If a player or a team is repeatedly disrespectful toward the other team, the ref calls a foul on them. This is a normal and well-received call.

10.3

Throwing on the gunshot, hard throws, and direct throws are competition, not disrespect. Opening a game you were handed with a direct throw is bad manners (2.5), and still not a foul.

Cite an article number in a dispute. The ref decides the rest.
